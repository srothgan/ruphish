Something feels wrong.
What next?

You do not need to prove a message is malicious before asking for help. Use these steps to respond to a suspected phishing attempt.

  1. Recognize
  2. Resist and report
  3. Delete
1

Recognize

Look for a request that does not fit the situation. These are prompts to check, not a complete list of what phishing looks like.

  • Pressure or an unexpected reward“Act now to avoid suspension” or “Claim your refund today.”
  • A request for sensitive information“Send your password” or “Tell me the verification code.”
  • An unexpected destination or fileA link leads somewhere unfamiliar, or a document arrives without the conversation you would expect.

Good grammar, familiar logos and a known sender do not guarantee safety. When uncertain, verify through a known contact or open the service independently.

2

Resist and report

Do not click links, open attachments, reply or call a number supplied by the suspicious message. Avoid its unsubscribe link too.

Use your organisation’s reporting method

Use the designated phishing-report action if available, or contact IT or security through a known channel. Follow their instructions for sharing the message. Reporting helps the team investigate and protect others.

3

Delete

After reporting, remove the suspicious message to avoid accidentally interacting with it later. If your reporting tool already removed it, there is nothing more to delete.

If IT or security asks you to preserve the message for investigation, follow those instructions before deleting it.

Already interacted? Report that too.

Stop and contact IT or security promptly. Explain whether you opened a file, entered information, approved a request or sent money, without including passwords or verification codes. Follow their recovery instructions.
